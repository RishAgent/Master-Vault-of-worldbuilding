# Felora Merit Credit Auction Economy

## 1. Core Principle

Felora maintains a strict distinction between:

- **Lifetime Merit** — permanent recognized accomplishment,
- **Merit Credits** — spendable institutional purchasing power,
- **Currency** — ordinary economic wealth.

The auction system used by noble Houses, Crown institutions, major guilds, and elite progression markets operates primarily through **Merit Credits (MC)**.

Important rule:

> **Lifetime Merit cannot be purchased.**

Buying Merit Credits with currency does **not** create Lifetime Merit.

Lifetime Merit remains proof of real accomplishment.

Merit Credits are simply a spendable institutional currency.

---

# 2. Base Merit Credit Valuation

The standard institutional valuation is:

> **10 Merit Credits = 1 Copper**

This creates the following exchange scale:

| Currency | Merit Credit Value |
|---|---:|
| 1 Copper | 10 MC |
| 1 Silver | 10,000 MC |
| 1 Gold | 10,000,000 MC |
| 1 Platinum Coin | 10,000,000,000 MC |
| 1 Platinum Paper | 10,000,000,000,000 MC |

This matches Felora's existing currency ladder:

- 1 Silver = 1,000 Copper
- 1 Gold = 1,000 Silver
- 1 Platinum Coin = 1,000 Gold
- 1 Platinum Paper = 1,000 Platinum Coins

---

# 3. Earned Merit Credits

When a person earns recognized Merit through verified accomplishments, they normally receive:

> **1 Lifetime Merit + 1 Merit Credit**

Example:

A person earns:

> **1,000,000 Merit**

Their record becomes:

- Lifetime Merit: +1,000,000
- Available Merit Credits: +1,000,000

The Merit Credits may later be spent.

The Lifetime Merit never decreases.

This creates two separate records:

> **Lifetime Merit = permanent status**

> **Merit Credits = spendable reward**

---

# 4. Buying Merit Credits With Currency

Currency may be exchanged into Merit Credits through:

- Merit Halls,
- Crown banks,
- major noble banks,
- royal auction houses,
- government-approved exchange institutions.

However, purchased Merit Credits are intentionally less efficient than earned Merit Credits.

The recommended purchase rate is:

> **1 Copper → 8 Merit Credits**

This represents a:

> **20% conversion loss / treasury premium**

Therefore:

| Currency Paid | Merit Credits Purchased |
|---|---:|
| 1 Copper | 8 MC |
| 1 Silver | 8,000 MC |
| 1 Gold | 8,000,000 MC |
| 1 Platinum Coin | 8,000,000,000 MC |

The institutional valuation still remains:

> **10 MC = 1 Copper**

The reduced purchase rate prevents wealthy nobles from completely bypassing the achievement economy.

---

# 5. Why the Conversion Penalty Exists

The Crown deliberately favors people who earn Merit through service and accomplishment.

A veteran who earns 40,000,000 MC through service possesses the equivalent auction purchasing power of:

> **4 Gold**

A wealthy noble who wants to purchase the same 40,000,000 MC using ordinary currency must spend:

> **5 Gold**

This creates a meaningful distinction between:

- people who are genuinely accomplished,
- people who are merely wealthy.

---

# 6. Merit Credit Redemption

If Merit Credits are redeemed back into ordinary currency, the standard institutional rate is:

> **10 Merit Credits → 1 Copper**

This preserves the base valuation.

However:

- most elite individuals prefer to keep Merit Credits,
- rare resources are often unavailable on ordinary currency markets,
- restricted auctions may only accept Merit Credits,
- some strategic purchases require earned Credits.

Merit Credits therefore often have greater practical value than their direct cash equivalent.

---

# 7. Why Noble Auctions Use Merit Credits

Elite auctions do not primarily sell ordinary goods.

Typical auction inventory includes:

- rare Monster Cores,
- pristine Mana Crystals,
- advancement catalysts,
- breakthrough medicines,
- ancient artifacts,
- bloodline resources,
- Beast Tamer resources,
- rare techniques,
- Gate keys,
- spatial artifacts,
- high-grade weapons,
- Gold Ring expedition resources,
- Platinum materials,
- unique inheritance items,
- rare elemental treasures,
- strategic magical resources.

These are resources that cannot simply be manufactured because someone has enough money.

The Merit Credit system ensures that strategic resources preferentially circulate toward:

- combat veterans,
- major contributors,
- high-Merit nobles,
- military officers,
- Crown servants,
- proven adventurers,
- Great Houses with real accomplishments.

# 8. Auction Lot Categories

## 8.1 Open Lot

Requirements:

- 100% purchased Merit Credits allowed.
- No earned-Credit requirement.
- Standard legal eligibility only.

Examples:

- ordinary enchanted equipment,
- common advancement medicines,
- low-grade artifacts,
- non-strategic materials.

## 8.2 Restricted Noble Lot

Requirements:

> **Minimum 10% of the winning bid must come from earned Merit Credits.**

Possible items:

- rare Silver resources,
- noble-grade equipment,
- advanced alchemical materials,
- high-value beast resources.

## 8.3 Strategic Advancement Lot

Requirements:

> **Minimum 25% of the winning bid must come from earned Merit Credits.**

Possible items:

- rare Silver breakthrough catalysts,
- Gold refinement materials,
- rare affinity cores,
- advanced techniques,
- major Beast Tamer resources,
- unique progression artifacts.

## 8.4 Crown Restricted Lot

Requirements:

> **Minimum 50% of the winning bid must come from earned Merit Credits.**

Additional requirements may include:

- Tier restrictions,
- social-standing requirements,
- Crown authorization,
- House sponsorship,
- military clearance,
- legal Class compatibility.

Possible items:

- national strategic resources,
- rare Gold or Platinum advancement treasures,
- unique Gate keys,
- ancient inheritance artifacts,
- restricted bloodline catalysts.

# 11. Alok's Merit Position

Alok has:

> **1,527,100,000 Lifetime Merit**

after synchronization and Grand Champion Merit recognition.

However:

> **Lifetime Merit is not spendable.**

His available Merit Credit balance must be tracked separately.

---

# 12. External Merit Synchronization

Alok's:

> **1,200,000,000 unsynchronized Merit**

was earned outside Wolfheide's fiscal authority.

Wolfheide may recognize the accomplishments for **Lifetime Merit** after verification without automatically granting the full amount as spendable Wolfheide Merit Credits.

Recommended conversion:

> **External Merit Credit Recognition Rate: 25%**

Therefore:

> 1,200,000,000 external Merit

may generate:

> **300,000,000 Wolfheide Merit Credits**

after verification.

This prevents Alok from instantly becoming absurdly liquid while still rewarding his legitimate external accomplishments.

---

# 13. Alok's Local Merit Credits

Merit earned directly under Wolfheide authority normally generates Credits at the standard:

> **1 Merit = 1 Merit Credit**

Therefore Alok's local Wolfheide achievements can contribute additional MC from:

- competition Merit,
- verified public service,
- Crown-sanctioned accomplishments,
- recognized monster kills,
- protection of civilians,
- major Gate contributions,
- verified military or political service.

His exact current spendable MC balance should be tracked separately from Lifetime Merit.

---

# 14. Why Alok Is Dangerous at Auction

Alok has several advantages:

- unusually high Lifetime Merit for his age,
- significant earned Merit Credits,
- access to wealth through tournament rewards,
- resource holdings,
- noble standing,
- Beast Tamer needs,
- high progression requirements,
- possible future Imperial compensation,
- access to restricted lots due to Tier and social status.

However, he should **not** be able to buy every item effortlessly.

That preserves tension.

A large bid such as:

> **480,000,000 MC**

should still matter to him.

This allows other:

- Dukes,
- royal Houses,
- Great Houses,
- foreign nobles,
- Gold-tier veterans

to compete meaningfully.

# 15. Auction Registration Explanation

A standard noble auction may explain the system as:

> "Lifetime Merit cannot be purchased."

> "Merit Credits can."

> "Ten Credits are valued at one Copper."

> "Currency conversion is subject to a twenty-percent treasury premium."

> "Strategic lots may require earned Credits."

---

# 16. Institutional Purpose

The Merit Credit auction system:

1. Rewards service and accomplishment.
2. Prevents wealth from completely overriding achievement.
3. Creates a controlled market for strategic progression goods.
4. Gives the Crown influence over rare-resource distribution.
5. Encourages nobles to perform real service.
6. Keeps Lifetime Merit separate from money.
7. Allows wealthy Houses to participate without making achievement irrelevant.
8. Creates natural tension between old money and proven veterans.

---

# 17. Canon Locks

- Lifetime Merit cannot be purchased.
- Lifetime Merit never decreases.
- Merit Credits are spendable.
- Currency may be exchanged into Merit Credits.
- Standard institutional valuation: **10 MC = 1 Copper**.
- Purchased exchange rate: **1 Copper = 8 MC**.
- Currency purchase therefore has a **20% premium/loss**.
- Earned Merit generally grants equal earned Merit Credits.
- Purchased MC does not increase Lifetime Merit.
- Open Lots allow fully purchased MC.
- Restricted Noble Lots require at least 10% earned MC.
- Strategic Advancement Lots require at least 25% earned MC.
- Crown Restricted Lots require at least 50% earned MC.
- Alok's external 1.2B Merit is recognized for Lifetime Merit.
- Recommended spendable-credit recognition for external Merit: **25%**, or **300M MC**.
- Alok's spendable Merit Credits remain separate from his 1.5271B Lifetime Merit.
- Noble auctions primarily use MC because they trade in scarce progression and strategic resources.
