# Gate Progression System — Spatial Scale, Population & Raid Structure

> [!summary]
> Gates are not merely dungeons.
>
> As Gate Tier rises, the enclosed space becomes larger, the ecosystem becomes more complex, the number of monsters increases dramatically, and clearing the Gate requires increasingly large coordinated expeditions.
>
> **Gate Tier determines the general power band of the ecosystem.**
>
> **Spatial Scale determines how large the pocket world is.**
>
> **Population Density and Ecosystem Type determine how many monsters actually inhabit it.**
>
> **Boss Structure determines the final threat.**
>
> These factors together determine real Gate difficulty.

---

# 1. Core Gate Principle

A Gate is a temporary or semi-stable dimensional environment connected to Earth through a portal.

Low-Tier Gates may resemble conventional dungeons.

Higher-Tier Gates increasingly resemble:

- valleys
- forests
- provinces
- kingdoms
- continents
- world-like pocket realms

A high-Tier Gate may contain:

- settlements
- migration routes
- rivers
- mountain systems
- ruins
- monster tribes
- intelligent civilizations
- multiple biomes
- subordinate Boss territories
- complex resource ecosystems

The term **Gate** refers to the portal and System classification.

The space beyond it may be vastly larger than the opening suggests.

---

# 2. Gate Tier

Gate Tier uses the same progression ladder as realized power:

- Ordinary
- Iron
- Bronze
- Silver
- Gold
- Platinum
- Diamond
- Supreme
- Noble
- Baron
- Count
- Duke
- King
- Emperor
- Sovereign
- Transcendent
- Demigod
- Divine

Each Tier follows:

**Beginner → Intermediate → Advanced → Peak**

before the next major Tier.

Gate Tier is not necessarily the exact Tier of every monster inside.

Instead, it defines the general ecosystem power band.

# 3. Standard Gate Scale Table

| Gate Tier | Approximate Spatial Scale | Typical Monster Population | Typical Raid Scale | Standard Final Boss |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Ordinary** | field / woodland / village-sized pocket | **~100** | 3–10 | Beginner Iron |
| **Iron** | large grassland / forest region / several km² | **~1,000** | 10–30 | Beginner Bronze |
| **Bronze** | district / large valley / small province | **~5,000–10,000** | 30–100 | Beginner Silver |
| **Silver** | province / small country | **~25,000–50,000** | 100–300 | Beginner Gold |
| **Gold** | small-to-medium country | **~100,000–250,000** | hundreds–1,000+ | Beginner Platinum |
| **Platinum** | large country / several countries | **~500,000–1,000,000** | thousands | Beginner Diamond |
| **Diamond** | continental region | **several million** | military-scale expedition | Beginner Supreme |
| **Supreme** | continent / multiple continents | **tens of millions** | national / multinational operation | Beginner Noble |
| **Noble+** | world-like pocket realms | **variable; potentially civilization-scale** | civilization-scale campaign | next-Tier ruler / unique entity |

> [!important]
> These are **typical population bands**, not mandatory exact values.

---

# 4. Population Scaling Philosophy

Monster population should rise aggressively with Gate Tier, but not by a rigid mathematical multiplier.

The key idea is:

> **Higher Gates are larger ecosystems, not merely stronger versions of smaller Gates.**

Population growth reflects:

- larger territory
- multiple biomes
- larger food chains
- settlements
- breeding populations
- intelligent monster societies
- subordinate armies
- regional Bosses
- greater Mana density

A Bronze Gate should feel meaningfully larger than an Iron Gate.

A Silver Gate should feel like an expedition into another country.

A Gold Gate should feel like entering a hostile kingdom.

---

# 5. Ecosystem Type Modifies Population

Gate Tier establishes the general power band, but **Ecosystem Type** can radically change population.

## Swarm / Horde Gate

Examples:

- insects
- undead
- goblins
- hive creatures
- rats
- locust-type magical beasts

Population:

**far above standard**

A Bronze swarm Gate could contain:

- 20,000
- 50,000
- or more

if most individual creatures are near the lower end of Bronze.

---

## Predator Gate

Examples:

- dragons
- wyverns
- great cats
- giant serpents
- apex beasts

Population:

**far below standard**

A Bronze Dragon Nest may contain only:

- a few hundred true combatants
- dozens of juveniles
- one or more major adults

because each creature is individually much stronger.

---

## Civilization Gate

Examples:

- goblin kingdom
- orc nation
- undead city-state
- intelligent beast settlement

Population may include:

- civilians
- workers
- noncombatants
- craftsmen
- soldiers
- elites by race name
- commanders
- rulers

Only actual hostile or System-valid combatants necessarily award combat EXP.

---

## Construct Gate

Examples:

- golems
- machines
- magical guardians

Population may be low while:

- individual durability is extremely high
- traps are numerous
- environmental hazards are severe

---

# 6. Standard Monster Composition

A normal Gate does not contain identical monsters.

A useful baseline distribution for the primary Gate Tier is:

| Substage | Approximate Share |
|---|---:|
| Beginner | **45%** |
| Intermediate | **30%** |
| Advanced | **17%** |
| Peak | **8%** |

This is a balancing baseline.

Actual ecosystems may differ significantly.

Example:

A 10,000-monster Bronze Gate may contain approximately:

- 4,500 Beginner Bronze
- 3,000 Intermediate Bronze
- 1,700 Advanced Bronze
- 800 Peak Bronze

plus the final Boss and possible special encounters.

# 10. Bronze Gate EXP Economy

Using the current Bronze EXP baselines:

| Stage | Typical EXP |
|---|---:|
| Beginner Bronze | 2,000–3,000 |
| Intermediate Bronze | 3,000–4,500 |
| Advanced Bronze | 4,500–6,750 |
| Peak Bronze | 6,750–10,000 |

For a standard **7,500-monster Bronze Gate** using the 45 / 30 / 17 / 8 distribution:

- 3,375 Beginner
- 2,250 Intermediate
- 1,275 Advanced
- 600 Peak

Using midpoint EXP values:

- 3,375 × 2,500 = **8,437,500**
- 2,250 × 3,750 = **8,437,500**
- 1,275 × 5,625 = **7,171,875**
- 600 × 8,375 = **5,025,000**

Approximate raw non-Boss EXP:

# **29,071,875 EXP**

before:

- Boss EXP
- clear rewards
- quests
- achievements
- multipliers
- contribution splits

> [!important]
> This does **not** mean every raider receives 29 million EXP.
>
> EXP is distributed according to contribution.

- kingdoms
- empires

# 18. Gate Raid Doctrine

A Gate may allow one person to enter alone.

That does **not** mean one person can realistically clear it.

The distinction is:

> **Solo entry is possible.**
>
> **Solo clearing becomes increasingly unrealistic as Gate scale rises.**

Why:

- enormous territory
- monster population
- multiple simultaneous threats
- time pressure
- injuries
- scouting requirements
- logistics
- Boss zones
- resource collection
- rescue operations
- camp defense
- environmental hazards

Even an overwhelmingly powerful Awakened may lack the time to physically search and clear an entire high-Tier Gate alone.

---

- frontline combat teams
- ranged teams
- mage groups
- healers
- scouts
- trap specialists
- logistics staff
- material recovery units
- reserve teams
- command staff
- Boss strike team
- emergency rescue team

---

# 20. Why Alok Can Still Progress Faster

Alok may separate from the primary raid force and fight alone inside a Gate.

Because EXP is contribution-based, this can allow him to earn far more EXP than an ordinary participant.

However, he is still not automatically clearing the entire Gate by himself.

He may personally:

- destroy thousands of monsters
- kill high-value targets
- eliminate regional Bosses
- assist multiple teams
- reach the final Boss early

while the larger expedition handles:

- mapping
- cleanup
- evacuation
- resource recovery
- other regions
- simultaneous threats

This preserves his exceptional progression without making large raid organizations meaningless.

---

# 21. Gate Boss Structure

Standard rule:

> **The primary final Boss is normally Beginner stage of the next major Tier.**

Examples:

- Ordinary Gate → Beginner Iron Boss
- Iron Gate → Beginner Bronze Boss
- Bronze Gate → Beginner Silver Boss
- Silver Gate → Beginner Gold Boss
- Gold Gate → Beginner Platinum Boss
- Platinum Gate → Beginner Diamond Boss
- Diamond Gate → Beginner Supreme Boss
- Supreme Gate → Beginner Noble Boss

Exceptional Gates may break this pattern.

---

# 22. Regional Bosses

Large Gates should often contain more than one major encounter.

Examples:

- territorial alpha
- fortress commander
- regional king
- broodmother
- tribal chief
- monster general
- guardian construct

These are not necessarily the final Gate Boss.

A Bronze or Silver Gate may contain several regional Bosses before the true final Boss.

---

# 23. Boss EXP

Boss status remains separate from ordinary stage EXP.

Current working ranges:

| Boss Tier | Typical EXP |
|---|---:|
| Iron | 1,000–2,500+ |
| Bronze | 25,000–60,000+ |
| Silver | 250,000–600,000+ |

Higher Boss EXP will be established when those Tiers become relevant.

Unique Gate rulers, Trial Bosses, and world Bosses may exceed these ranges significantly.

# 25. Gate Duration

Gate clear time scales with size.

Typical examples:

| Gate Tier | Typical Clear Time |
|---|---|
| Ordinary | hours |
| Iron | hours to 1–2 days |
| Bronze | 1–5 days |
| Silver | several days to weeks |
| Gold | weeks |
| Platinum | weeks to months |
| Diamond+ | campaign-scale |

These are only baseline expectations.

---

# 26. Gate Failure Risk

Higher Gates threaten more than individual raiders.

Potential consequences include:

- Gate break
- monster escape
- civilian casualties
- regional destruction
- national emergency
- cross-border crisis

Gold and above may become geopolitical events.

Supreme and above may become civilization-level threats.

---

# 27. Core Difficulty Formula

A Gate's actual danger should be understood as:

# **Gate Tier**
+
# **Spatial Scale**
+
# **Population**
+
# **Population Density**
+
# **Ecosystem Type**
+
# **Boss Structure**
+
# **Environmental Hazards**
+
# **Intelligence / Civilization Level**

No single number completely describes a Gate.
