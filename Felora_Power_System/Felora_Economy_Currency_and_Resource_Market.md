# Felora Economy, Currency and Resource Market

> [!summary]
> Felora uses one unified currency system, but different social classes operate at radically different scales.
>
> **Copper** dominates ordinary civilian life.
>
> **Silver** dominates professional, military, urban and lower-noble life.
>
> **Gold** dominates capital noble circles, major magical transactions and high-Tier progression.
>
> **Platinum** and **Platinum Paper** are strategic currencies used by great Houses, royal treasuries and major organizations.

---

# 1. Currency Ladder

| Currency | Conversion |
|---|---:|
| Copper | Base everyday coin |
| **1 Silver** | **1,000 Copper** |
| **1 Gold** | **1,000 Silver** |
| **1 Platinum Coin** | **1,000 Gold** |
| **1 Platinum Paper** | **1,000 Platinum Coins** |

Optional local small change:

**100 Copper Bits = 1 Copper**

# 2. Currency by Social Layer

## Rural Commoners

Primary currency:

**Copper**

Used for:

- food
- basic clothing
- simple tools
- repairs
- low-grade medicine
- local taxes
- village services

Silver is meaningful and uncommon.

Gold is practically absent from ordinary village life.

## Towns and Professional Households

Primary currency:

**Copper and Silver**

Common users include:

- soldiers
- artisans
- merchants
- registered Merit holders
- smiths
- alchemists
- caravan operators
- officials

## Capital and Noble Circles

Primary currency:

**Silver and Gold**

Silver is routine noble spending money.

Gold is used for:

- major properties
- elite equipment
- rare monster resources
- advancement packages
- noble contracts
- dowries
- large trade agreements
- high-grade magical materials

## Great Houses and Royal Economy

Primary currency:

**Gold and Platinum**

Used for:

- territorial purchases
- military procurement
- fortress construction
- strategic resources
- Gold/Platinum advancement resources
- inter-kingdom contracts

## Kingdom-Scale Economy

Primary currency:

**Platinum Coins and Platinum Paper**

Platinum Paper is an ultra-high-value magically authenticated treasury note rather than ordinary cash.

# 3. Commoner Income Baseline

| Household Type | Approximate Annual Income |
|---|---:|
| Poor rural family | 40–60 Copper |
| Stable commoner family | 80–120 Copper |
| Successful farmer / artisan | 150–300 Copper |
| Wealthy village merchant | 300–700 Copper |
| Major rural landholder | 1+ Silver |

A village household earning:

**5–10 Copper per month**

is considered reasonably stable.

A family can survive on roughly:

**100 Copper per year**

if it is not making major land purchases, paying unusual debts, or purchasing magical resources.

---

# 4. Meaning of Silver and Gold

Because:

**1 Silver = 1,000 Copper**

one Silver may equal roughly a decade of ordinary rural household income.

Because:

**1 Gold = 1,000 Silver = 1,000,000 Copper**

Gold is fundamentally a noble and high-Tier denomination.

A rural family could live for generations without ever personally owning a Gold coin.

---

# 5. Monster Resource Valuation

Felora's resource economy follows the same broad exponential logic as Tower valuation.

For internal design:

> **1 TP of standardized material value ≈ 1 Copper of Feloran baseline commodity value.**

This is only a worldbuilding conversion.

Tower Points and Feloran currency are not directly exchangeable.

Actual prices vary with:

- purity
- affinity
- scarcity
- region
- wartime demand
- species
- integrity
- mutation
- buyer specialization

# 7. Monster Core Market Values

Monster Cores are normally more valuable than equivalent Mana Crystals because they carry both Mana and biological/magical imprint.

Working baseline:

> **Comparable Monster Core ≈ roughly 2× equivalent Mana Crystal**

| Tier | Typical Feloran Value |
|---|---:|
| Ordinary | 10–40 Copper |
| Iron | 100–300 Copper |
| Bronze | 1–3 Silver |
| Silver | 10–30 Silver |
| Gold | 100–300 Silver |
| Platinum | 1–3 Gold |
| Diamond | 10–30 Gold |
| Supreme | 100–300 Gold |

Rare affinities can push values far above baseline.

Examples:

- Lightning
- Blood
- Frost
- Flame
- Shadow
- Space
- Regeneration
- Dragon
- rare bloodline properties

---

# 8. Merit, Money and Loot Are Separate

Felora distinguishes:

> **Merit = recognized accomplishment**
>
> **Money = economic wealth**
>
> **Loot = physical resource ownership**

Buying a Monster Core does not grant the Merit of the person who killed the monster.

A person may be wealthy but low-Merit.

A highly meritorious person may be cash-poor after major advancement spending.

---

# 9. Registered Merit Holder Economy

Registered Merit holders have much greater expenses than ordinary civilians.

Major costs include:

- advancement materials
- cores
- Mana crystals
- medicine
- catalysts
- armor
- weapons
- repairs
- training
- storage artifacts
- formations
- breakthrough facilities
- travel

Their salary matters, but successful monster-resource income can be far greater.

# 13. Baronial Economy

A normal established Baron may control roughly:

# **500 Silver per year**

in dependable estate revenue.

Sources can include:

- land rents
- local taxes
- monster-processing rights
- Crown stipend
- trade duties
- resource rights
- estate businesses
- military allowances

## Typical Annual Baronial Expenses

| Expense | Approximate Cost |
|---|---:|
| Household servants | 40–80 Silver |
| Guards / retainers | 80–150 Silver |
| Estate maintenance | 50–100 Silver |
| Food / hosting / clothing | 30–60 Silver |
| Transport / storage / animals | 20–40 Silver |
| Crown obligations / taxes | 30–70 Silver |
| Gifts / social obligations | 20–50 Silver |
| Advancement / equipment reserve | 50–150+ Silver |

A Baron earning 500 Silver may have only:

# **~100–200 Silver**

of genuinely flexible annual wealth after normal obligations.

# 15. Capital Noble Economy

Within the capital:

- Silver is normal respectable spending money.
- Gold is major-transaction money.
- Copper is economically insignificant to wealthy Houses.

Common noble expenses include:

- servants
- guards
- tutors
- magical education
- transport
- social events
- fine clothing
- hospitality
- medicine
- advancement resources
- political gifts

Even rich noble Houses can face financial strain because high-Tier advancement is extremely expensive.

---

# 16. Social Mobility Through the Monster Economy

A commoner may progress:

farmer's child
→ military service
→ Iron
→ monster-resource shares
→ Silver savings
→ Bronze
→ better missions
→ greater Merit and wealth
→ Peak Bronze
→ Baronetcy

A single Bronze Monster Core may be worth more than many years of the person's childhood household income.

This is one reason combat achievement can transform social class.

---

# 17. Noble Structural Advantage

Nobles can supply:

- tutors
- equipment
- protected hunting access
- medicine
- advancement materials
- storage artifacts
- escorts
- superior facilities
- political permissions

However:

> **Money can buy resources.**
>
> **Money cannot directly buy Lifetime Merit.**

The Merit Path therefore remains a real achievement system.

# 19. Canon Locks

- 1 Silver = 1,000 Copper.
- 1 Gold = 1,000 Silver.
- 1 Platinum Coin = 1,000 Gold.
- 1 Platinum Paper = 1,000 Platinum Coins.
- 5–10 Copper/month is a decent rural family income.
- ~100 Copper/year can support a modest rural household.
- Copper dominates commoner life.
- Silver dominates professional, military, urban and lower-noble life.
- Silver and Gold dominate noble circles in the capital.
- Gold is major noble and high-Tier transaction currency.
- Platinum and Platinum Paper are strategic currencies.
- Merit and monetary wealth are separate.
- Monster Cores and Mana Crystals form the backbone of the progression-resource economy.
- Monster Cores are normally worth roughly twice equivalent Mana Crystals.
- A normal established Baron may control roughly 500 Silver/year in dependable revenue.
- Servants, guards, estate costs, politics and advancement consume a large portion of noble income.
- Advancement is one of the largest economic pressures on combatants and noble Houses.
