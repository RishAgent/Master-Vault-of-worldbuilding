# Felora Social Standing and Nobility

Felora has a genuine commoner–noble divide, but the divide can be crossed through personal achievement.

The social ladder is determined by a combination of:

- **Tier** — realized personal power
- **Lifetime Merit** — recognized accomplishment
- **Formal Promotion** — legal recognition by the Crown or relevant authority
- **Inherited Status** — family standing, which remains separate from personal qualification

# 2. Why the Noble Thresholds Expand Sharply

Everything through **Crown Champion** represents increasingly prestigious commoner or gentry-level standing.

The major social wall begins at:

> **Crown Champion — 5,000,000 Merit**

to:

> **Baronet — 50,000,000 Merit**

This tenfold jump is intentional.

Becoming hereditary nobility is not treated as the next routine promotion.

It represents entry into a legally privileged social class with:

- hereditary House recognition
- estate rights
- succession rights
- greater legal protections
- better institutional access
- eligibility for noble administration
- stronger political influence

---

# 3. Baronetcy

A normal Baronet qualification requires:

- **Peak Bronze Tier**
- **50,000,000 Lifetime Merit**
- formal recognition by the Crown or appropriate noble authority

A Baronet may gain:

- House recognition
- hereditary surname/status
- estate rights
- limited noble privilege
- stronger legal standing
- access to noble institutions
- succession eligibility

Baronetcy is the first true hereditary noble step.

---

# 4. Baron Qualification

A normal Baron qualification requires:

- **Beginner Silver Tier**
- **500,000,000 Lifetime Merit**
- formal Crown approval
- legal ennoblement
- usually a land or territorial grant

A Baron is no longer merely socially recognized.

A Baron may govern actual territory and is expected to maintain:

- local defenses
- guards and retainers
- Gate response
- roads and infrastructure
- taxes and administration
- military obligations
- Crown service

---

# 5. Higher Noble Thresholds

After Baron, the Merit ladder continues to expand:

> **Baron — 500M**  
> **Viscount — 1.5B**  
> **Count — 5B**  
> **Marquis — 15B**  
> **Duke — 50B**  
> **High Duke — 150B**  
> **Grand Duke — 500B**  
> **Realm Pillar — 1.5T**

This prevents major noble titles from being gained through a handful of lucky monster hunts.

These standings represent decades, centuries, or longer periods of recognized achievement for normal Felorans.

---

# 6. Inherited Title vs Personal Merit

Birth status and personal achievement are separate.

A Duke's child may be:

- a ducal heir by blood
- personally only Iron-tier
- low in Lifetime Merit
- legally unable to exercise full ducal authority

The parent's Tier and Merit are never inherited.

---

# 7. Succession

Major titles require personal qualification to exercise full authority.

If an heir is underqualified:

- a regent may govern
- the heir receives a qualification period
- failure to qualify may cause the territorial title to revert to Crown review
- a qualified relative, House member, or outside candidate may replace them

Possible regents include:

- surviving spouse
- qualified relative
- House elder
- Crown appointee

---

# 8. Noble Advantages

Nobles retain major structural advantages:

- tutors
- training halls
- advancement materials
- family techniques
- superior equipment
- Gate access
- academies
- political connections
- bloodline resources
- protected hunting grounds
- contracted beasts
- better medical care
- access to elite institutions

However, money and birth cannot directly purchase Lifetime Merit.

---

# 9. Merit Path Principle

A commoner may legally rise through society by accumulating both power and recognized accomplishment.

Typical progression:

> Common Citizen  
> → Registered Meritor  
> → Proven Meritor  
> → Distinguished Meritor  
> → Crown Retainer  
> → Banner Retainer  
> → Senior Banner  
> → Crown Champion  
> → Baronet  
> → Baron  
> → higher nobility

This preserves social mobility without making noble status easy.

---

# 10. Alok's Position

Alok currently possesses the power requirement for Baron because he is already:

> **Beginner Silver**

However, his Merit is far below the noble threshold.

Current noble requirement:

> **Baron — 500,000,000 Lifetime Merit**

Therefore Alok can be vastly stronger than his legal social position.

This mismatch is intentional.

His Tier reflects power.

His Merit reflects recognized local accomplishment.

His title only changes after both qualifications are met and formal promotion is granted.

---

# 11. Social Principle

> **Blood grants opportunity.**
>
> **Tier grants capability.**
>
> **Merit grants recognized accomplishment.**
>
> **The Crown grants legal authority.**

# 12. Canon Locks

- All thresholds through Crown Champion remain unchanged.
- Baronet now requires **50,000,000 Lifetime Merit**.
- Baron now requires **500,000,000 Lifetime Merit**.
- Viscount requires **1,500,000,000**.
- Count requires **5,000,000,000**.
- Marquis requires **15,000,000,000**.
- Duke requires **50,000,000,000**.
- High Duke requires **150,000,000,000**.
- Grand Duke requires **500,000,000,000**.
- Realm Pillar requires **1,500,000,000,000**.
- Major noble titles require both Tier and Merit.
- Formal promotion is still required after qualification.
- Inherited title and personal Merit remain separate.
- Underqualified heirs may require regency and can lose territorial authority if they fail to qualify.
