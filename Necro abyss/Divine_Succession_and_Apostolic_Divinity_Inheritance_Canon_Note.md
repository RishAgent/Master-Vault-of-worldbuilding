# Divine Succession and Apostolic Divinity Inheritance

> [!important] Canon Scope
> This note locks the working rules for **Divinity inheritance, Divine Power inheritance, Divine-Born eligibility, Severed Apostles, and divine-status stripping**.
>
> This mechanic applies to the upcoming Necro Abyss confrontation and to future divine conflicts unless later explicitly superseded.

---

# 1. Core Principle

Divinity is not merely energy.

It is a metaphysical quality tied to:

- divine existence;
- divine authority;
- Law compatibility;
- godly physiology;
- divine soul architecture;
- and the ability to contain and circulate Divine Power.

When a being possessing **true Divinity** dies, part of that Divinity and part of its remaining stored Divine Power may pass to a qualifying killer.

This process is called:

# **DIVINE SUCCESSION**

---

# 2. Standard Divine Succession Rule

When a qualifying being personally kills another being that possesses true Divinity:

# **30% of the victim's recoverable remaining Divinity and stored Divine Power may be inherited by the qualifying killer.**

Working rule:

> **Inherited Divinity = 30% of recoverable Divinity remaining at death**

> **Inherited Divine Power = 30% of recoverable stored Divine Power remaining at death**

This percentage applies to what remains in the victim at the time of death.

It does **not** automatically grant:

- 30% of everything the victim ever possessed;
- 30% of their total historical divine strength;
- 30% of their entire divine domain;
- 30% of all faith energy ever accumulated;
- or 30% of the original power of a dead patron god.

Only the **recoverable divine reserves still present in the slain being** are eligible.

---

# 3. Where the Remaining 70% Goes

The remaining divine essence is not automatically transferred to the killer.

Depending on the victim and circumstances, it may:

- dissipate into the surrounding Divine Realm;
- return to compatible Laws;
- scatter into divine domains;
- remain inside relics;
- remain inside temples or faith networks;
- become unusable residue;
- be absorbed by surviving divine structures;
- disperse into ambient divine energy;
- or become inaccessible.

Therefore Divine Succession is intentionally inefficient.

Killing a god is not a perfect power-transfer method.

---

# 4. Eligibility — Who Can Receive Divine Succession?

A being must possess a vessel and soul capable of retaining true Divinity.

## Standard Eligible Beings

Normally:

- Gods
- True Divine beings
- beings with stable divine physiology
- beings recognized as possessing a compatible divine existence

may inherit Divine Succession.

---

# 5. Ordinary Mortals

Ordinary mortals cannot naturally retain true Divinity.

If an ordinary mortal kills a divine being:

- the divine essence does not permanently integrate;
- excess Divinity disperses;
- Divine Power destabilizes;
- the mortal may be injured, overwhelmed, or simply unable to retain it.

Ordinary mortals therefore do **not** qualify as full Divine Successors.

---

# 6. Divine-Born Exception

Divine-Born are the major mortal exception.

Alok and Celestia are mortal beings.

They are **not gods**.

However, their bodies and souls are naturally capable of containing true Divinity.

Therefore:

# **DIVINE-BORN QUALIFY FOR DIVINE SUCCESSION**

Known qualifying Divine-Born:

- **Alok Aeonmorta**
- **Celestia**

They are currently the only known mortals capable of naturally receiving and retaining inherited Divinity without first becoming gods.

This reinforces the unique nature of Divine-Born:

> They are mortals capable of carrying what should normally belong only to gods.

---

# 7. Divine-Born Do Not Need Godhood to Hold Divinity

A Divine-Born does not need to ascend to godhood before possessing Divinity.

Therefore:

- Alok may possess Divinity while remaining mortal;
- Celestia may possess Divinity while remaining mortal;
- both may gain additional Divinity through Divine Succession;
- both may use Divinity as part of their personal growth without being classified as gods.

This is one of the reasons Divine-Born are feared by established divine factions.

---

# 8. Divine Power vs Divinity

These remain distinct resources.

## Divinity

Divinity represents:

- divine nature;
- divine authority potential;
- compatibility with divine skills;
- ability to contain Divine Power;
- divine soul architecture;
- higher-order existence.

## Divine Power

Divine Power is the usable energy generated, accumulated, stored, transferred, or consumed through divine systems.

A slain divine being may therefore leave behind both:

- **Divinity**
- **stored Divine Power**

The 30% inheritance rule applies separately to both.

---

# 9. Severed Apostles

The five dead gods left behind True Apostles.

These Apostles remain Supreme-tier beings.

Their patrons are dead.

Therefore their Apostleship is:

# **SEVERED**

Working condition:

[ APOSTOLIC STATE: SEVERED ]

[ DIVINE PATRON: DECEASED ]

[ AUTHORITY LINK: COLLAPSED ]

[ MIRACLE ACCESS: LOST ]

[ ACTIVE DIVINE REINFORCEMENT: LOST ]

[ DIVINE POWER REGENERATION: 0 OR NEAR-ZERO ]

[ STORED DIVINITY: PRESENT ]

[ STORED DIVINE POWER: PRESENT ]

[ DIVINE AUTHORITY FRAGMENTS: PRESENT ]

---

# 10. What Severed Apostles Retain

A Severed Apostle may still retain:

- Supreme-tier body;
- old divine blessings;
- sacred techniques;
- apostolic physiology;
- divine relics;
- fragments of authority;
- inherited Divine Skills;
- ancient combat experience;
- faith-fed reserves;
- accumulated Divinity;
- accumulated Divine Power;
- patron-derived traits already fused into the body or soul.

They remain extremely dangerous.

---

# 11. What Severed Apostles Lose

Because their patron god is dead, they lose:

- active Divine Power replenishment;
- direct Law reinforcement;
- real-time divine guidance;
- miracle intervention;
- emergency god descent;
- active patron protection;
- continuous authority amplification;
- full use of patron-linked Divine Authority;
- unrestricted replenishment of apostolic Divinity.

Their remaining divine reserves are effectively:

# **STRANDED DIVINE CAPITAL**

They possess it.

They may use parts of it.

But they cannot properly replenish or fully regenerate what is spent.

---

# 12. Killing a Severed Apostle

If Alok or Celestia personally kills a Severed Apostle, Divine Succession applies.

Example:

A Severed Apostle possesses at death:

- **40,000 Divinity**
- **600,000 stored Divine Power**

A qualifying Divine-Born killer receives:

- **12,000 Divinity**
- **180,000 Divine Power**

This is 30% of what remains in the Apostle.

It is **not** 30% of the original patron god's power.

---

# 13. Five Dead Gods — Five True Apostles

For the Necro Abyss assassination confrontation, the preferred canon structure is:

# **Five surviving True Apostles**

One principal Apostle for each of the five gods erased by infant Alok.

This gives a clean thematic parallel:

> Five gods attacked Alok as an infant.

> Five gods were erased.

> Five surviving Apostles later come to finish their masters' final command.

> They instead become Divine Succession targets for the Divine-Born they tried to kill.

---

# 14. Divine Attribution Rule

Full 30% Divine Succession requires the killer to be recognized as the direct qualifying slayer.

Valid examples include:

- direct physical kill;
- direct spell kill;
- direct Divine Skill kill;
- direct artifact kill;
- direct Corpse Explosion triggered by the qualifying killer;
- direct Sovereign-authority execution performed by the qualifying killer.

The kill must be attributable to the qualifying being.

---

# 15. Shadow / Legion Kill Rule

Kills performed entirely by:

- Shadows;
- Legion soldiers;
- allied soldiers;
- independent retainers;
- summoned units;
- subordinates acting without the qualifying killer's direct finishing action

do **not automatically grant the full 30% Divine Succession**.

Minor residue may still pass through Alok's Sovereign system where appropriate, but the major Divine Succession transfer requires Alok or Celestia to be the recognized direct killer.

This preserves the importance of personally finishing high-value divine targets.

---

# 16. Lesser Divine Residue

Not every follower possesses true Divinity.

Ordinary divine followers may carry only:

- blessings;
- relic energy;
- trace faith essence;
- tiny divine residue;
- weak authority fragments;
- temporary patron energy.

When such followers die, Alok may absorb or recover trace divine resources through:

- Soul Space;
- Eternal Cycle;
- Divine Power conversion;
- battlefield collection;
- relic extraction.

However, this does **not** count as full Divine Succession.

Millions of minor followers may collectively provide meaningful resources, but they should not individually create major Divinity gains.

---

# 17. True Apostle Reward Priority

The five True Apostles are the primary Divine Succession targets.

Recommended battle logic:

1. Corpse Explosion annihilates most Gold and Platinum followers.
2. Shadow and Legion forces wipe out large numbers of survivors.
3. Diamond executioners are killed or captured.
4. The five Supreme Apostles survive the opening destruction.
5. Their Severed state is revealed.
6. Alok and Celestia personally kill them.
7. Divine Succession activates.
8. Their trapped Divinity and Divine Power pass partially to the Divine-Born.

---

# 18. Celestia Also Qualifies

Celestia is not merely present as a companion.

She is also a Divine-Born.

Therefore if she personally kills one of the Apostles:

# **she receives Divine Succession independently**

---

# 19. Shared Kill Rule

If Alok and Celestia jointly kill the same divine target, the 30% recoverable share may be divided according to:

- final authority ownership;
- decisive contribution;
- direct finishing action;
- soul-recognition;
- or a negotiated System allocation.

Default working rule:

> The being recognized as the **primary slayer** receives the full 30% succession share.

Shared distribution is possible but is not automatic.

---

# 20. Divine Skill / Authority Inheritance

Divine Succession automatically transfers:

- Divinity;
- Divine Power.

It does **not automatically transfer**:

- full Laws;
- full divine domains;
- every Divine Skill;
- complete memories;
- patron identity;
- divine titles;
- priesthoods;
- temples;
- worship networks.

However, compatible fragments may survive separately as:

- Authority Fragments;
- Divine Skill remnants;
- Law shards;
- relic-bound inheritance;
- domain fragments;
- soul memories.

These require separate analysis, assimilation, or compatibility.

---

# 21. Divine Status Is Not Automatically Inherited

Killing a god does not automatically make the killer that god's replacement.

Alok does not become:

- God of the victim's domain;
- owner of all their worshippers;
- owner of all their temples;
- lawful successor to their divine throne

unless an additional mechanic explicitly grants that inheritance.

Divine Succession transfers recoverable divine resources, not automatic political or cosmological office.

---

# 22. Seraphae's Punishment of Lennoxxarh

Seraphae's punishment of Lennoxxarh directly interacts with this mechanic.

Her intent is not merely to kill him.

She intends to strip him of:

- Divinity;
- Divine Power;
- divine authority;
- divine status;
- divine resurrection mechanisms;
- divine existence classification.

She forces him to repeatedly destroy and expend his own divine state until:

[ DIVINITY: 0 ]

[ DIVINE POWER: 0 ]

[ DIVINE AUTHORITY: LOST ]

[ DIVINE STATUS: REVOKED ]

[ EXISTENCE CLASSIFICATION: MORTAL ]

Only then will her followers kill him.

---

# 23. Why Seraphae Does This

Seraphae is deliberately denying Lennoxxarh a divine death.

If he died while still divine, possible consequences could include:

- Divine Succession;
- surviving divine remnants;
- Law fragments;
- relic formation;
- reincarnation routes;
- divine-domain residues;
- inheritance by another god;
- continuation through worship;
- preserved divine dignity.

Seraphae denies him all of it.

Her punishment means:

> Lennoxxarh does not die as a god.

> He dies as a powerless mortal who remembers having been one.

This is intended to be crueler than ordinary execution.

---

# 24. Divine Death vs Mortal Death

## Divine Death

A divine being killed while still possessing Divinity may leave:

- recoverable Divinity;
- Divine Power;
- Authority Fragments;
- Law residue;
- relics;
- domain remnants;
- succession opportunities.

## Mortal Death

A former divine being reduced fully to mortality before death leaves none of the normal divine inheritance associated with god-death.

This distinction is now important canon.

---

# 25. Why Divine-Born Terrify Gods

The Divine Succession mechanic strengthens the setting reason Divine-Born are feared.

A Divine-Born can:

- remain mortal;
- naturally possess Divinity;
- create or use Divine Power;
- kill divine beings;
- inherit part of their Divinity;
- grow without first entering the normal godhood hierarchy;
- potentially accumulate divine resources from multiple divine sources.

The existential danger is not merely that Divine-Born are talented.

It is that they can grow **outside the ordinary divine order**.

---

# 26. Alok-Specific Interaction

Alok is especially dangerous because Divine Succession combines with:

- Sovereign of Eternal Cycle;
- Soul Space;
- Life / Death authority;
- self-generated Divine Power;
- self-created Divine Skills;
- soul capture;
- Divine-Born physiology;
- Eternal Legion;
- Class Codex;
- Divine Power storage.

Therefore a slain divine target may provide Alok with multiple distinct gains:

1. soul;
2. memories;
3. Divinity;
4. Divine Power;
5. Authority fragments;
6. relics;
7. usable corpse or remains;
8. Shadow extraction where valid;
9. information;
10. future Legion potential where appropriate.

These systems remain separate and should not be merged into one automatic reward.

---

# 27. Celestia-Specific Interaction

Celestia is also a valid Divine Successor.

Her restored Divine-Born soul and body allow her to contain:

- Divinity;
- Divine Power;
- compatible divine fragments.

She may therefore independently grow through divine kills.

This supports her future development without making her dependent on Alok for every form of progression.

# 30. Canon Locks

- **Divine Succession** is now a real setting mechanic.
- A qualifying killer may inherit **30% of a slain divine being's recoverable remaining Divinity and stored Divine Power**.
- The 30% applies only to what remains at the moment of death.
- The remaining ~70% is normally lost, dispersed, returned to Laws, retained in relics, or otherwise unavailable.
- Ordinary mortals cannot naturally retain true Divinity.
- **Divine-Born are the mortal exception.**
- **Alok and Celestia qualify for Divine Succession.**
- Divine-Born do not need to become gods before possessing Divinity.
- Divinity and Divine Power remain separate resources.
- The five dead gods' True Apostles remain **Supreme-tier Severed Apostles**.
- Severed Apostles retain stored Divinity and Divine Power.
- Severed Apostles cannot properly replenish patron-derived Divine Power after their gods' deaths.
- Their remaining divine reserves are effectively stranded.
- Killing a Severed Apostle may trigger Divine Succession.
- The preferred Necro Abyss structure uses **five True Apostles**, one representing each dead god.
- Full Divine Succession requires the qualifying being to be recognized as the direct or primary slayer.
- Shadow, Legion, or ally kills do not automatically grant the full 30% succession share.
- Lesser followers may provide trace divine residue but do not normally trigger major Divine Succession.
- Divine Succession does not automatically transfer full Laws, divine domains, titles, worship networks, or every Divine Skill.
- Compatible Authority fragments and Divine Skill remnants may survive separately.
- Killing a god does not automatically make the killer the god's political or cosmological successor.
- Seraphae's punishment of Lennoxxarh deliberately strips him of Divinity and Divine Power before death.
- A being stripped completely of Divinity and divine status dies as a mortal and does not produce normal divine succession rewards.
- Divine-Born are feared partly because they can accumulate divine resources while remaining outside the ordinary godhood hierarchy.
- Alok's Divine Succession rewards remain separate from soul capture, Shadow extraction, Legion restoration, memory acquisition, relic recovery, and Authority-fragment assimilation.
