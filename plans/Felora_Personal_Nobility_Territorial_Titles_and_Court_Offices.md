# Felora Personal Nobility, Territorial Titles and Court Offices

> [!summary]
> In Felora, **personal noble standing does not automatically grant land**.
>
> A person may legally be a Baron, Viscount, Count, Marquis, or Duke through the established combination of Tier, Lifetime Merit, and formal recognition without personally ruling matching territory.
>
> Territorial rule is a separate Crown or liege grant.

---

# 1. Four Separate Systems

Feloran society distinguishes:

## Personal Noble Standing
The individual's legally recognized rank based on:
- Tier
- Lifetime Merit
- formal promotion

Examples:
- Baronet
- Baron
- Viscount
- Count
- Marquis
- Duke

## Territorial Title
Administrative authority over land.

Examples:
- Barony
- Viscounty
- County
- Marquisate
- Duchy

Territorial authority may include taxation, roads, guards, Gate response, courts, infrastructure, military obligations, and succession duties.

## Inherited / House Status
A person may be born into a noble House without personally possessing the Tier or Merit required for the House's ruling title.

The parent's:
- Tier
- Merit
- personal standing
- accomplishments

are never inherited.

## Institutional Office
A person's official role.

Examples:
- Royal Knight
- Court Mage
- Guard Captain
- Royal Alchemist
- Palace Guardian
- Gate Commander
- Intelligence Officer
- Household Knight
- Ducal Retainer
- First Sword

Office is separate from personal noble standing.

---

# 2. Landless Nobility

A person may hold personal noble standing without governing land.

Examples:

### Personal / Landless Baron
- Beginner Silver
- 500M+ Lifetime Merit
- formally recognized as a Baron
- no independent Barony
- may serve as a Guard Captain, Royal Knight, Court Mage, or great-house retainer

### Landed Baron
- personally qualified as a Baron
- additionally granted a Barony
- responsible for territory, taxation, defense, roads, Gate response, and administration

The same distinction can exist for Viscounts, Counts, Marquises, and Dukes.

---

# 3. Service Under Higher Nobility

A Viscount may employ a personally noble Baron.

Example:

## Viscount
- Personal Standing: Viscount
- Tier: Intermediate Silver
- Territory: Viscounty
- Role: ruler

## Household Guard Captain
- Personal Standing: Baron
- Tier: Beginner Silver
- Territory: none
- Role: Guard Captain

The Baron remains personally noble but is institutionally subordinate to the Viscount.

---

# 4. Royal Family Retainers

The royal family may employ many personally noble retainers.

Examples include:
- Baron-tier Royal Knights
- Viscount-tier Court Mages
- Count-tier Guard Commanders
- Marquis-tier Royal Guardians
- Duke-tier national retainers

These people may possess enormous Tier and Merit while holding no independent territory.

Their influence comes from:
- personal standing
- royal appointment
- court access
- military or magical office

---

# 5. Royal Court Examples

## Royal Court Knight
- Personal Standing: Baron
- Tier: Beginner Silver
- Merit: 500M+
- Territory: none
- Office: Royal Court Knight

## Royal High Mage
- Personal Standing: Viscount
- Tier: Intermediate Silver
- Merit: 1.5B+
- Territory: none
- Office: Royal High Mage

## Commander of the King's Guard
- Personal Standing: Count
- Tier: Advanced Silver
- Merit: 5B+
- Territory: none
- Office: Commander of the King's Guard

## Royal Guardian
- Personal Standing: Marquis or Duke
- Tier: Peak Silver or Gold
- Territory: none
- Office: Personal Guardian of the King

# 6. Why Powerful People Choose Service Instead of Land

Not every high-Merit individual wants territorial government.

Royal or great-house service may provide:
- superior training
- rare advancement resources
- Gate access
- royal libraries
- political protection
- restricted techniques
- research opportunities
- military command
- greater access to high-value Merit missions

Some powerful people prefer:
- combat
- magic
- research
- exploration
- court service
- military command

over governing civilians and collecting taxes.

---

# 7. Great House Retainers

The same rule applies to major Houses such as:
- House Starlight
- House Evantior
- other Ducal Houses
- Marquis Houses
- major Count Houses

A Duke may maintain:
- Baron retainers
- Viscount retainers
- Count retainers
- senior Marquis-level retainers

without granting them independent territory.

---

# 8. Territory, Rank, and Office Are Not the Same

A landed Baron may govern more land than a landless Count.

However:
- the Count still outranks the Baron socially,
- the Baron has more direct territorial authority inside his own Barony,
- a royal Count may possess far greater political influence through proximity to the Crown.

Likewise, a Guard Captain may be a better fighter than the Viscount they serve without outranking them politically.

---

# 9. Inheritance

A hereditary heir may hold a claim to territory without yet qualifying personally.

Example:
- child of a Duke
- personally only Iron or Bronze
- insufficient Merit for Duke standing

Possible consequences:
- regency
- qualification period
- Crown review
- replacement by a more qualified relative or appointee

Inherited claim and personal qualification remain separate.

# 11. Social Principle

> **Tier grants capability.**
>
> **Merit grants recognized accomplishment.**
>
> **Formal promotion grants personal standing.**
>
> **Territory grants administrative authority.**
>
> **Office grants institutional authority.**
>
> **Blood grants inheritance and opportunity.**

These overlap but are not interchangeable.

---

# 12. Canon Locks

- Personal noble standing does not automatically grant territory.
- Baron, Viscount, Count, Marquis, and Duke may exist as personal legal standings without matching land grants.
- A Baron may serve as a Viscount's Guard Captain.
- A Viscount may serve the Crown as a Court Mage without holding a Viscounty.
- A Count may command the King's Guard without ruling a County.
- The Crown may employ personally noble retainers of very high Tier and Merit.
- Great Houses may employ personally noble retainers.
- Institutional office, territory, inherited status, and personal noble standing are separate.
- Powerful landless nobles are socially normal and legally recognized.
- Not every high-Merit individual wants territorial governance.
- Heirs do not automatically inherit Tier, Merit, or personal qualification.
